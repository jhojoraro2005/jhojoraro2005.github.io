💡 What Is email-engine, In Plain Words

Your email inbox is constantly changing. A new message arrives. Someone replies. You move something to a folder. You mark it as read. Most programs see those changes as a blur — they refresh the screen and the details are lost.

email-engine writes all of it down instead. Every arrival, every reply, every change becomes a numbered entry in a log — like a receipt tape that never tears. Because the tape is complete, you can look back at any moment, replay a sequence, or undo something cleanly.

It speaks three of the most common email languages — IMAP (used by almost every mail server), JMAP (a newer, faster standard) and inbound SMTP (the way mail is delivered). It can also fire webhooks, which are automatic notifications sent to other programs the instant something happens.

The short version: you point it at a mailbox, and you get a tidy, ordered stream of everything that occurred — plus a way to react to it and take it back if you change your mind.

🔐 Privacy and Safety, Explained Simply

Because email-engine runs on your machine, there is no outside company holding a copy of your inbox. That is what "self-hosted" means: the software is yours, the data is yours, and the door has your lock on it.

The scoped tokens are the other half of that promise. When you let an AI assistant use your mailbox, you do not hand over your master password. You create a token that permits exactly one narrow thing — for example, reading new arrivals but never deleting anything. If you stop trusting that assistant, you delete the token and its access dies immediately.

Reversible actions mean the engine always knows how to put things back. If a rule you wrote moves 300 messages somewhere odd, you can undo the batch rather than repairing it by hand.

🤖 Using It With AI Assistants

The engine includes an MCP server. MCP stands for Model Context Protocol — think of it as a standard plug shape that AI assistants already know how to use. Whenis switched on, an assistant can ask your mailbox questions through the engine instead of through raw credentials. It sees events, not your password. You decide which tokens exist and what each one may do, and you can revoke any of them at any time.

A typical use: you tell the assistant "summarise anything urgent that arrived today." It asks email-engine for today's events through its token, reads the ones it is allowed to read, and answers. It cannot wander into last year's archive or delete a thread, because the token does not allow it.
